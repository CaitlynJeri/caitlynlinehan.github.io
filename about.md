Who I am. I'm Caitlyn Linehan, a PhD candidate in Geography at UC Santa Barbara, where I work in the SPAR Lab under Dr. Trisalyn Nelson. I expect to finish in June 2027, and I teach GIS and human geography at Riverside City College.

What I do. I study how people move through cities on foot and by bike, and who gets left out of that. Most of my work sits at the intersection of pedestrian safety, walkability, and access to parks and green space. Day to day I work in ArcGIS Pro and ArcGIS Online, Python (arcpy, geopandas, pandas), R for statistical modeling, and QGIS when I need something open source. Lately I've been training a segmentation model to pull crosswalk locations out of aerial imagery, which means a lot of time in PyTorch and a lot of time hand-labeling tiles.

Where it came from. I grew up in the Bronx, where I walked everywhere before I ever thought of walking as a research subject. The question I couldn't drop was why some streets are built like people are expected to be on them and some are not.

Where I'm going. I'm finishing a dissertation on green space access and pedestrian infrastructure, and I'm aiming for a role where I keep doing applied spatial research on transportation equity, either in a faculty position or with a public agency or research group doing safety analysis.
